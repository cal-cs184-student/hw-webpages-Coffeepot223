Part 1: Masses and Springs

For Part 1, I built the initial mass-spring system by first generating an evenly spaced grid of point masses across the cloth's dimensions, while ensuring vertical orientations included a small random Z-offset to break symmetry for future self-collisions. I then populated the springs vector by iterating over the grid and applying structural, shearing, and bending constraints to connect adjacent, diagonal, and secondary masses using 1D array indexing.

Part 2: Simulation via Numerical Integration

Implementation Overview

In Part 2, I computed the total force acting on each point mass by accumulating external forces, such as gravitational and spring-correction forces, with Hooke's Law. I then used Verlet integration with a damping factor to calculate the new positions of the point masses over time. Then, I applied Provot's deformation constraints to ensure no spring stretched beyond 10% of its rest length.

Effects of Spring Constant (k_s)

A low spring constant makes the cloth highly elastic, causing it to stretch and ripple continuously as it falls to rest. Conversely, a high k_s creates a more rigid material that resists bending and settles into its resting state much faster with minimal bouncing.

Effects of Density

Decreasing the density makes the cloth much lighter, resulting in a gentle fall that comes to rest with smooth surface folds. Increasing the density makes a heavier cloth that plummets quickly, pulling heavily on the pinned corners and creating creases at rest.

Effects of Damping

A low damping percentage causes the system to retain most of its energy, leading to swinging and bouncing that takes a very long time to stabilize. A high damping percentage rapidly dissipates energy, forcing the cloth to fall slowly and come to an immediate stop almost instantly without any extra swinging.

Part 3: Handling Collisions with Other Objects

Handling Collisions Implementation

To handle collisions, I implemented intersection tests for both spheres and planes to determine if a point mass breached the primitive's surface during the current time step. If an intersection occurred, I then calculated the exact tangent point on the surface and a correction vector from the mass's previous position to a target slightly above this tangent point. Finally, I updated the current position using this correction vector scaled down by friction.

Differences in Results (Sphere)

With a lower ks (500), the cloth is highly elastic and stretches deeply around the sphere, conforming tightly to its circular shape. At the default ks (5000), it drapes naturally with standard folds, while a high ks (50000) makes the cloth extremely stiff, causing it to balance rigidly on top of the sphere with minimal downward draping.

Part 5: Shaders

Shaders & Blinn-Phong Model

A shader program is an isolated program running in parallel on the GPU, where vertex shaders first transform geometric properties like positions and normals, passing them as varyings to fragment shaders that, in turn, compute the final per-pixel color. The Blinn-Phong shading model calculates this final color by combining a constant ambient light component, a diffuse reflection component that scatters light evenly across the surface, and a specular component that creates bright highlights based on the viewer's specific angle.

Bump vs. Displacement Mapping

Bump mapping creates the visual illusion of surface detail by modifying the normal vectors within the fragment shader without actually altering the object's physical geometry. In contrast, displacement mapping physically modifies the vertex positions in the vertex shader according to a height map, which actually changes the object's geometry and reacts much more noticeably when the sphere's mesh resolution is increased.
